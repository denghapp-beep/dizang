Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva's Teaching: Is the Death Penalty Good or Evil?

Through protecting life, punishment and compassion, the author's question on capital punishment.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: for criminals whose crimes are utterly heinous — murderers and others whose acts destroy victims' families — does the Bodhisattva approve of the death penalty under worldly law? And how does the Bodhisattva view the death penalty, and the many countries in Europe and America that oppose it?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"Punishment in the world exists to stop the many evils, not to repay grievance. When the wicked commit crimes that fill the multitude with fear and destroy human bonds and righteousness, those who protect beings should set grave precepts to cut off the harm. Yet punishment should be carried out with compassion, not with anger; the act of cutting off evil is to protect the many, not to gratify oneself. Some nations abolish it to show mercy; some nations keep it to protect the multitude. Neither is ultimate; each follows what suits the capacities, conditions and hearts of its people.

I do not hold the death penalty to be good, nor do I hold its abolition to be good. Goodness lies in 'being able to spare beings from suffering'; evil lies in 'carrying it out from hatred'. Cut off one evil person, and hundreds and thousands of good are saved; protect one evil life, and hundreds and thousands of sufferings are added. Therefore, if the death penalty is carried out, guard the compassionate heart; if it is abolished, be watchful of its harm. Only remember this: the law puts stopping evil first; the heart takes compassion as its root."

🔶Plain Rendering

The world sets up punishment to stop people's wrongdoing and keep society at peace, not to satisfy revenge and resentment.

If a wrongdoer commits monstrous crimes that leave the public in fear and destroy human bonds and justice,

then those responsible for protecting the welfare of beings should establish strict laws and penalties to fully prevent further harm.

However, when punishment is carried out, it should start from compassion, not be driven by hatred;

measures to cut off evil are meant to protect the greater number of people, not to let a few (including victims' families or the public) vent their anger for a moment.

Some countries abolish the death penalty to show compassion and tolerance toward life; some countries keep it to protect the safety of the community with the strongest means.

Neither approach is the ultimate solution to the evil in human nature; both are expedient measures chosen according to each society's people, history and public will.

I (the Bodhisattva) do not regard carrying out the death penalty as "good" in itself, nor do I regard abolishing it as "good" in itself.

True "good" lies in whether it can truly spare beings from suffering; true "evil" lies in inflicting punishment out of hatred in the heart. If punishing one evil person brings peace to hundreds and thousands of good people; if sparing one evil person's life would bring new suffering to hundreds and thousands —

therefore, if the death penalty is chosen, be sure to keep the compassion that arises for protecting beings; if it is abolished, be sure to guard carefully against the real harm it may bring.

You need only remember this one fundamental principle: the law's first function is to stop wrongdoing; the human heart's foundation is compassion and mercy.
