No.38

If Cause, Effect and Karma Are Already Fixed, What Can a Good Thought and Diligent Practice Still Change?

Old causes are already sown, yet today we can still add new ones — asking how practice changes direction.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: if cause, effect and karmic force are already fixed, then what, in the end, can a being's single good thought and diligent practice in this moment still change? Please, compassionate Bodhisattva, give a teaching.

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"Good man,
you ask:
'If cause, effect and karmic force are already fixed,
what can a being's present thought of good
still change?'"
"Know this:
if karma were wholly fixed,
the Buddhas would not need to teach the Dharma;
the Bodhisattvas would not need to deliver beings;
and there would be no meaning in beings' practice."

"Cause and effect is not fatalism;
cause and effect is causes and conditions."
"Past causes
can become today's effects;
today's causes
will in turn become the effects of the future."
"Beings think
effects cannot be changed;
yet they do not know
that effects already ripened
may be hard to turn completely,
while effects not yet ripened
can be turned by present causes."

"It is like a seed in the soil
not yet sprouted;
water it diligently with sweet dew,
and the good shoot will grow;
let the weeds run wild,
and the good shoot will be smothered."
"A single thought of compassion
does not erase old causes,
but it can grow new ones."

"A single thought of repentance
does not change yesterday,
but it can change tomorrow."
"So the practitioner
does not struggle against cause and effect,
but parts from ignorance."
"To know karma without fearing karma,
to trust causes and diligently plant causes —
this is called right view."

"Your single thought toward good today
is the beginning of tomorrow's light."
(End of teaching.)
