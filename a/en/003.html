No.03

The Truth About "Not Being Able to Let Go"

Amid wealth, reputation and family love, practicing love without binding and protection without control.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

One day in a dream I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: could you teach the world what attachment is? How do you see people's attachment to wealth, power, desire, status — and the attachment of mothers and fathers to their children?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"The attachment of beings is like a fly clinging to honey: sweet at the first taste, stuck fast thereafter. To be attached is to cling to the self, and to cling to what the self possesses. If one knows that all things arise from conditions and are impermanent, there is nothing to gain and nothing to lose. Wealth is but a brief gathering, power but a passing shadow, beauty but a borrowed form, fame but a sound on the wind. A parent's love for a child is born of affection too; without understanding causes and conditions, love becomes a bond. If you can love without binding and protect without controlling, then affection becomes compassion and the bond is released. What the world calls 'not being able to let go' is not that the thing is heavy — it is that the heart is heavy. If the heart can turn, iron chains become flowers, and the sea of suffering becomes a boat."

🔶Plain Rendering

The Bodhisattva said: the attachment of worldly people is like a fly craving honey. At first it tastes sweet, but once you are stuck, it is hard to get free. To be "attached" really means being bound by "me" and "mine" — treating the self and everything we own as solid and unchanging.

If we understand that everything comes together through causes and conditions and is always changing, we will see that both gaining and losing are only illusions. Wealth is just something gathered for a while; power is a brief shadow; beauty is the outer appearance of a borrowed body; fame is no more than wind passing the ear.

As for parents' love for their children — it is the deepest bond in the human world. But without understanding causes, conditions and impermanence, this love turns into a restraint. If you can love without binding and protect without controlling, that deep affection turns into compassion, and the bond that once restrained it naturally comes undone.

People often say "I can't let go." In truth, it is not that the thing is too heavy, but that the heart is too heavy. If you can shift your mind — turning attachment into compassion and worry into blessing — the chains that once bound you become flowers, and even the sea of suffering becomes a boat that carries yourself and others across.
