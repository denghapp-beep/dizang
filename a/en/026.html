No.26

How Rare Is a Human Birth, Really?

Bringing the question of "the rarity of human birth" back to cherishing and using this life.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: in hearing the Dharma we often hear that a human body is hard to obtain, yet most people feel nothing about it. Could the Bodhisattva teach the world how difficult it really is, how much merit is needed, and how low the chance is of being born human?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"A human body is hard to gain —
not because it is scarce,
but because the moment of gaining it is hard."
"Miss by a hair's breadth,
and one turns to other paths;
one thought astray,
and one goes very far."
"The evil paths are easy to fall into,
the good paths hard to keep;
with slender merit,
one cannot become human."

"Having gained a human body,
it is not by chance;
it is the gathering of good causes,
formed over a long time."
"To gain it and not know it
is a great loss;
to know it and use it well
is to live up to it."

🔶Plain Rendering

The preciousness of a human body is not that "few beings get to be human", but that you become human at the right time, under the right conditions. At the threshold of life and death, the difference is often only a hair's breadth; if a thought goes astray, the next life will not be a human one. It is easy to fall into the three lower realms (hell, hungry ghosts, animals), but staying in the good realms of humans and devas takes great merit. Without enough merit, one cannot be human at all. Since you have obtained a human body, it is not by accident; it was achieved by good causes gathered over a long time. To have a human body and not know how precious it is, is the greatest loss. Now that you know, use this life well to practice, do good and awaken — only then will you be worthy of this rare blessing.
