No.27

Reciting "Amituofo" or "Namo Amituofo"?

On the four- and six-character forms of the Buddha's name: beyond the number of words, attending to gathering the mind and continuity.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: when reciting the Buddha's name, is it better to say "Namo Amituofo" or "Amituofo"? In recent times the elder Dharma Master Chin Kung said the four characters are enough, but the patriarchs and great masters of earlier generations advocated the six-character great name.

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"The Name is one in essence;
the number of characters is no obstacle."
"Four characters or six,
the merit is not two;
in sound they differ,
in the heart they are the same."
"If the mind scatters easily,
the simpler is more convenient;
if you wish to gather the mind,
six characters are good as well."

"Follow your own habits,
let the mind continue,
let it not be interrupted —
this is the essential point."

🔶Plain Rendering

Line-by-line rendering: "The Name is one in essence; the number of characters is no obstacle." The essence of the Buddha's name is one (Amitabha Buddha); how many characters there are is no obstacle. "Four characters or six, the merit is not two." The merit gained from four characters or six is no different. "In sound they differ, in the heart they are the same." Though the sounds recited differ, in the heart (focus, reverence, faith and vow) they are the same. "If the mind scatters easily, the simpler is more convenient."

If your mind wanders easily and has many idle thoughts, the simpler four characters are more convenient (easier to keep going).

"If you wish to gather the mind, six characters are good as well." If you want to collect your thoughts and help yourself focus, six characters are also very good. "Follow your own habits, let the mind continue without interruption — this is the essential point."

Follow your own habits and capacities, and let the mind of recitation continue without breaking off — that is what matters most.
