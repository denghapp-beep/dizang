No.15

What Is Time?

From past, present and future, an inquiry into the relation between thought and time.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked the Bodhisattva: what is time? What is time, really?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"Time is not truly existent. Beings cling to it as three periods, saying there is past, present and future. Yet in the nature of reality there is no beginning and no end; all realms are simultaneous, and through the differences of conditions, flowing change appears. If the mind chases thoughts as they arise, it sees time as a line; if the mind enters oneness, it sees no boundary. Therefore time is only a provisional name, not a real dharma."

🔶Plain Rendering

Time is not something that "truly exists"; it is only a concept we perceive through the differences between conditions and the continuous flow of thoughts.

• Ordinary people see time as a line of past — present — future, but this is because our thoughts arise and pass one after another, so we feel time is flowing.

• From the standpoint of the nature of reality, time has no beginning and no end; all realms exist at once.

• When our mind runs after one thought after another, it sees the world as a timeline; when our mind rests in a state of non-discrimination and oneness, it sees a reality without boundaries, without before or after.

• So time is only a provisional name set up by people for convenience, not an ultimately real dharma.
