No.55

Did the Buddha Oppose Mantras?

What the Buddha rejected was living by spells that deceive the many; reciting mantras to gather the mind and protect life must not be confused with wrong livelihood — yet reciting mantras is not awakening.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I humbly ask:

Sutra 189 of the Madhyama Agama, the Discourse on the Noble Path, speaks of "sustaining one's life by wrong livelihood through various kinds of 'animal spells'." Yet in later Mahayana sutras there are also records of the Buddha teaching dharanis and mantras. Was the Buddha originally opposed to mantras themselves, or to using spells to pursue worldly gain and make a living? And what exactly are "animal spells"?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"Good man,
you read the Madhyama Agama
and saw the Buddha speak of 'animal spells'
and sustaining life by wrong livelihood.
Then you looked at the Mahayana teachings
and saw dharanis and mantras
also handed down in the Buddha's tradition.
A doubt arose in your heart:
both are mantras —
why is one rejected
and the other upheld?

I now urge you:
in studying the Buddha's way, discern the meaning;
do not cling only to the name.
The Dharma has precepts,
has concentration,
has wisdom.
It also has skillful means,
establishing many gates of practice
according to the capacities of beings.

Yet every skillful means
must be judged by where it leads.
If spells are used
to divine fortune and misfortune,
to frighten beings,
to crave offerings,
or, in the name of the miraculous,
to deceive people and take their money,
then even if the mouth speaks of the Buddha's Dharma,
you should examine it carefully.

Though the name invokes the Buddha,
if the conduct turns against the Way,
do not take it as the true Dharma because of its name.
Yet if sutras and mantras are recited
to hold right mindfulness,
to remember the Three Jewels,
to vow to protect life,
and to nurture compassion,
neither should it, merely because
it bears the name of mantra,
be confused with wrong livelihood.

But you must understand:
reciting mantras is not awakening;
spiritual responses are not liberation.
If you recite a mantra a thousand times
and greed and anger remain as before;
if you seek protection
but do not cultivate goodness,
then reflect
whether your practice has strayed from its purpose.

You also ask:
what are 'animal spells'?
Do not, seeing the word 'animal',
assume they are
spells recited by animals.
The meaning here
must be carefully examined
according to the context of the sutra
and the sense of the words in ancient India.

Do not look only at the written words
and decide what they refer to.
In reading sutras, seek the original meaning;
in practice, examine the heart.
And do not, because
later ages have dharanis,
think that what the early teachings
rejected
is no different at all.

Nor, because
the early teachings criticized spells,
conclude that all
dharani practice
is a false teaching.
Between the two
there is a distinction of precepts
and also a difference in the development of the teachings.

Investigate it as it truly is;
do not force them to be the same,
nor lightly declare them different.
Good man,
if you ask
what the Buddha truly valued,
look at it through right livelihood,
right view,
freedom from greed,
and the path out of suffering.

Nourish wisdom with the Dharma,
not greed with arts;
guard the heart with precepts,
not confuse the many with marvels.
Do not, because of one line of scripture,
lightly slander other schools;
do not, because of one passage of mantra,
neglect the precepts.

May you uphold the sutras
without losing discernment;
may you practice the Dharma
without losing right view.
Know what is said,
and know still more what it points to;
discern the skillful means,
and discern its purpose too.

Do not cling to words and quarrel;
rely on the true Dharma and seek clarity."

🔶Plain Rendering

Good man, you read the Madhyama Agama and saw the Buddha speak of "animal spells, sustaining life by wrong livelihood". Then you saw that the Mahayana sutras record dharanis and mantras, which have also been passed down in the Buddhist tradition. So a question arose in your mind: they are both mantras — why was one rejected by the Buddha, while the other is recited by Buddhists?

I now urge you:
when studying Buddhism, you should discern the meaning,
and not cling only to the name.
The Dharma has precepts, concentration and wisdom.
It also has skillful means,
setting up many ways of practice
according to the capacities of different beings.
But every skillful means
should be judged by its direction and purpose.

If spells are used
to tell fortunes,
to scare people,
to crave offerings,
or, in the name of the supernatural,
to deceive people and cheat them of money,
then even if the person talks about the Buddha's teaching,
you should look carefully.

Even if it is called Buddhist,
if the behavior goes against the right path,
you must not treat it as the true Dharma just because of its name.
But if reciting sutras and mantras
is for gathering right mindfulness,
remembering the Three Jewels,
vowing to protect life,
and growing compassion,
then you also must not, just because
it is called a mantra,
lump it together with wrong livelihood.

But you must understand:
reciting mantras is not the same as awakening;
spiritual experiences are not the same as liberation.
If you recite a mantra a thousand times
and your greed and anger are just the same;
if you ask the Buddha for protection
but do not do good,
then you should reflect on
whether your practice has drifted from its true purpose.

You also ask:
what are "animal spells"?
Do not see the word "animal"
and assume they are spells recited by animals.
The meaning here
must be examined carefully
according to the context of the sutra
and the meaning of the words in ancient India.

You should not just look at the words
and decide what they refer to.
When reading sutras, look for the original meaning;
when practicing, look at your own heart.
Also, do not,
because later generations have dharanis,
assume that what the early teachings
rejected
is no different at all.

And do not,
because the early teachings criticized spells,
conclude that all
dharani practice
is a false teaching.
Between the two
there are distinctions in the precepts
and differences in how the teachings developed.

You should investigate them as they truly are,
without forcing them to be the same,
and without lightly saying they are completely different.
Good man,
if you ask
what the Buddha truly valued,
look at it through right livelihood, right view,
and the path of leaving greed and leaving suffering.

Use the Dharma to grow wisdom,
not magic arts to grow greed;
use the precepts to guard your heart,
not strange displays to mislead people.
Do not, because of one line of scripture,
lightly slander other traditions;
do not, because of one passage of mantra,
neglect the precepts.

May you hold to the sutras
without losing your ability to think clearly;
may you practice the Dharma
without losing right view.
Know what is said,
and even more, know what it points to;
discern the skillful means,
and also discern its purpose.

Do not cling to words and start arguments;
follow the true Dharma and seek understanding.
