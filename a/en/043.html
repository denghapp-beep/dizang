No.43

What Is Emptiness?

Through flowers blooming and falling and clouds gathering into rain, reading the images of dependent arising and emptiness.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: what is emptiness? I humbly ask the Bodhisattva to teach everyone.

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

When beings hear of emptiness, most grow afraid, thinking emptiness means that nothing at all exists. So they lose its meaning and fall into the view of annihilation. Emptiness is not non-existence; it is empty of self-nature. Things exist dependent on conditions; when conditions scatter, they cease. A flower blooming is not permanent; a flower falling is not annihilation. Clouds gather into rain; when the rain is spent, it returns to emptiness. Cling to existence, and you give rise to craving; cling to non-existence, and you give rise to despair.

To leave both existence and non-existence is called the Middle Way. Knowing emptiness without forsaking beings, compassion grows ever wider; awakening to emptiness without leaving the world, cause and effect remain clear. If you see the meaning of emptiness, you do not destroy the ways of the world; if you dwell in the appearance of emptiness, you lose true emptiness instead. Therefore the practitioner should contemplate all dharmas: dependently arisen, empty by nature; empty by nature, dependently arisen — the two are without difference.

🔶Plain Rendering

When beings hear the word "emptiness", most become afraid; they think "emptiness" means everything is gone, cut off into nothing. So they lose the true meaning of emptiness and fall into the wrong view of annihilation. "Emptiness" does not mean "nothing exists"; it means that nothing has an independent, unchanging self-essence. Phenomena exist by depending on causes and conditions; when the conditions disperse, the phenomena disperse too. A flower blooms, but it does not stay forever (it is not permanent); a flower falls, but it is not completely wiped out into nothingness (it will bloom again next year).

Clouds gather and form rain; after the rain falls, it returns to emptiness. Because you cling to "existence", you develop craving; because you cling to "non-existence", you develop despair. Leaving both extremes — clinging to existence and clinging to non-existence — is called the Middle Way. Knowing emptiness does not mean giving up compassion; rather, compassion becomes even wider; realizing emptiness does not mean leaving the world; cause and effect remain perfectly clear. If you truly see the meaning of emptiness, you will not undermine the laws by which the world works;

if you cling to an "image of emptiness" (thinking emptiness is a kind of state or realm), you lose true emptiness instead.

So practitioners should observe that all phenomena
are "dependently arisen and empty by nature";
and because they are "empty by nature", they can "arise dependently".
These two are not two different things, but two sides of one whole.
