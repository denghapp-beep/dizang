No.49

Is Human Nature Originally Good, Originally Evil, or Does It Turn With Conditions?

Not fixing people as good or evil, but observing habit, environment and the choice in this moment.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: Question: is human nature originally good, originally evil, or does it turn according to conditions?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

If you say human nature is originally good,
how can beings
give rise to greed, anger and delusion?
If you say human nature is originally evil,
how can the wicked
also repent in a single thought?
So the two words good and evil
cannot wholly define the hearts of beings.

The hearts of beings
arise with conditions,
move with habit,
and turn with what they are steeped in.
When a thought of greed arises,
one may do evil;
when a thought of compassion arises,
one may also do good.

When good thoughts are often cultivated,
good roots gradually grow;
when evil thoughts are often practiced,
evil karma gradually deepens.
So today's good person,
if they do not guard the heart,
may also do evil;
today's evil person,
if they can know their faults,
may also turn to good.

People are not born forever good,
nor born forever evil;
the heart arises with conditions,
and conduct is formed by thought.
Yet you must not therefore say:
'Since all follows conditions,
good and evil have no meaning.'
Precisely because the heart can turn,
practice has meaning.

If everything were fixed from birth,
the evil forever evil,
the good forever good,
what need would there be for teaching?
What need for repentance?
What need for practice?
Beings have ignorance,
so they create evil;
beings also have the nature of awareness,
so they can know evil and change.

Water has no fixed form:
in a square vessel it is square,
in a round vessel it is round;
yet if water is muddied,
it can be made clear again.
The heart is the same.
The teaching of the family,
the friends one keeps,
the surroundings one lives in,
one's own habits,
one's past experiences —
all can become conditions.

But that conditions influence you
does not mean conditions
decide everything for you.
When circumstances arrive,
you still have one thought
with which to choose.
When someone hurts you,
you can take revenge,
or you can stop the evil and not take revenge;
when someone deceives you,
you can learn their deceit,
or you can, because of it,
keep faith all the more.

So practice is not waiting
for good conditions to make you good,
but, when conditions appear,
learning to choose good.
If you ask:
'Should I then trust human nature?'
Do not believe that beings
will never do evil;
nor believe that beings
can never change.

Knowing people can do evil,
you have the wisdom to protect;
knowing people can do good,
you do not abandon compassion.
Seeing an evil person,
guard against their evil,
without cutting off
the possibility that they will change;
seeing a good person,
respect their goodness,
without holding them up
as one who never errs.

Seeing people this way,
you will have less disappointment
and less rigidity.
What the Dharma values
is not arguing
whether human nature should be labeled
'good' or 'evil',
but asking you:
this thought, right now —
where will it go?

Thought after thought
becomes habit;
habit, over a long time,
forms the person;
if a person can become aware,
habit too can be turned.
Therefore I say:
good and evil are not set in stone,
conditions are not fate;
the hearts of beings can turn,
and so beings can be delivered.

🔶Plain Rendering

If human nature were originally good, why would beings still give rise to greed, anger and delusion? If human nature were originally evil, why can even wicked people repent in a single moment? So the two words "good" and "evil" cannot fully define the hearts of beings.

Beings' hearts arise with conditions, are pulled by habits, and change with the influences they absorb day by day.

When a greedy thought arises, it may lead to a harmful act; when a compassionate thought arises, it may also lead to a good act. When good thoughts are cultivated again and again, good roots slowly grow; when harmful thoughts are repeated again and again, harmful karma gradually deepens. So a good person today, if they do not guard their heart, may also do wrong; a person who does wrong today, if they can recognize their faults, may also turn toward good. People are not born forever good, nor born forever evil; the heart moves with conditions, and actions are formed by thoughts.

But you cannot therefore say: "Since everything follows conditions, good and evil have no meaning." It is precisely because the heart can change that practice has meaning. If everything were fixed at birth — the wicked forever wicked, the good forever good — why teach? Why repent? Why practice? Because beings have ignorance, they do wrong; because beings also have the nature of awareness, they can recognize wrong and change.

Just as water has no fixed shape — in a square container it becomes square, in a round container it becomes round —

yet if water is muddied, it can also be made clear again. The heart is like this too. Family upbringing, the friends we keep, the environment we live in, our own habits and our past experiences can all become conditions. But that conditions influence you does not mean they decide everything for you. When a situation comes, you still have one thought with which to choose. When someone hurts you, you can choose revenge, or choose to stop the harm and not take revenge; when someone deceives you, you can learn to deceive others, or become even more trustworthy because of it.

So practice is not waiting for good conditions to make you good, but learning to choose good when conditions appear. If you ask: "Should I trust human nature, then?" do not believe that beings will never do wrong; and do not believe that beings can never change. Knowing that people can do wrong, you have the wisdom to protect yourself; knowing that people can do good, you do not give up compassion.

When you see someone doing wrong, guard against their wrongdoing, but do not cut off the possibility that they will change;

when you see a good person, respect their goodness, but do not treat them as someone who will never make a mistake.

Seeing people this way, you will have less disappointment and less rigid prejudice. What the Dharma values is not arguing over whether human nature should be labeled "good" or "evil", but asking you: where will this thought, right now, go? One thought after another becomes habit; habits built up over time form a person's character; if a person can become aware, habits can also be changed. So I say: good and evil are not set in stone, and conditions are not fate; beings' hearts can change, and so beings can be delivered.
