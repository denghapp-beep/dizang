No.44

After a Loved One Passes, Is Missing Them a Form of Attachment?

There is no need to force ourselves to forget those we love; let longing walk alongside going on with life.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: may I trouble the Bodhisattva to teach all beings — "After a loved one passes away, is missing them a kind of attachment?"

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

When a loved one departs, what sin is there in longing?
Remembering their kindness, the heart is moved;
recalling their voice and face, tears fall of themselves.
This is not attachment; it is human feeling.
If longing does not obstruct, if remembering still lets you act,
the deeper the longing, the more the good heart grows.
If you cling to their form and will not let them go,
if you resent impermanence and suffer day after day,
this is clinging to appearances.

So know this: longing may remain; attachment may be released. To love a person, you need not imprison your own heart. To remember their kindness, you may continue their goodness. If you turn longing into cultivating blessings and dedicating merit, then within sorrow there is a vow, within pain there is compassion. Though a person has left the world, the bond is not wholly broken; the form departs but the kindness remains, the appearance departs but the goodness endures. Do not force yourself to forget the one you love; nor hold your grief and never let it go.

Let the longing come, let the tears flow;
when they have flowed, still walk on.
This is letting go — not forgetting.

🔶Plain Rendering

When a loved one has passed away, is missing them some kind of wrong? Remembering their kindness, the heart is naturally moved; recalling their voice and face, tears naturally fall. This is not attachment; it is real human feeling between people. If missing them does not stop you from living normally and still lets you keep going, then the deeper you miss them, the more your good heart will grow. If you cling to their past form and will not allow them to truly leave; if you resent impermanence and trap yourself in pain every day — that is what "clinging to appearances" is.

So know this: you may miss them; you can let go of attachment. To truly love someone, you do not have to lock your heart in the past. Remembering their kindness, you can carry on the goodness they left behind. If you can turn this longing into the strength to cultivate blessings and dedicate merit, then there will be a vow within the sorrow, and compassion within the pain. Although the person has left, the bond is not completely broken; the body is gone, but the kindness remains; the appearance has vanished, but the good deeds remain. Do not force yourself to forget the one you love; but do not cling tightly to grief either.

Allow the longing to come, allow the tears to flow;
once they have flowed, keep walking forward.
This is letting go, not forgetting.
