"Without Living Through Another's Pain, Do Not Urge Them to Be Good; Had You Lived Through Mine, You Might Not Be as Good as Me" — Is This Against Buddhist Thought?

Facing another's suffering, reflecting on the measure of understanding, persuasion and compassion.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: how does the Bodhisattva see this saying — "Without living through another's pain, do not urge them to be good; had you lived through my pain, you might not be as good as me"? Is it against Buddhist thought?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"This saying is not wholly wrong, yet it stops at ordinary feeling. To see suffering and hold back from urging is the wisdom of self-protection; to know suffering and give rise to compassion is the practice of a Bodhisattva. If you make your own suffering a gate that shuts others out, then suffering becomes self-attachment, not a cause of liberation."

🔶Plain Rendering

This saying is not completely wrong, but it stays at the level of ordinary people (based on self-protection and emotional reaction).

Seeing others suffer and choosing not to persuade or interfere is a wisdom of self-protection (a worldly wisdom).

Knowing the true nature of suffering and therefore giving rise to compassion, willing to help beings leave suffering, is the practice of a Bodhisattva.

If you use your own suffering as an excuse to shut others out and refuse to grow, that suffering becomes a solid "self-attachment" instead of a condition for liberation.
