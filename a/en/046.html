No.46

Telling the Truth Is Not Always Good; a Lie Is Not Always Evil

When truth and kindness seem to conflict, reflecting on speech, timing and a heart that does not deceive.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: "Bodhisattva, does a white lie told so as not to hurt someone break the precept against false speech? If truth and compassion conflict, which should come first?"

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

The precept against false speech does not only examine true or false; it also observes the heart. If you knowingly say what is false with a deceiving heart, causing others harm, this is false speech.

If it is to protect someone, so they do not suffer, with no heart to deceive or harm, and the words are a skillful means — here you must discern carefully.

True words can wound, false words can also harm; between compassion and truth, do not lightly choose one and discard the other.

If true words are spoken only to vent, though the words are true, it is not compassion.

If kind words are not to deceive, not for your own gain, but only wishing to stop suffering, then consider skillful means.

If you can speak the truth without wounding, speak the truth; if you can say it later, say it later; if you can say less, say less; you need not state every truth outright.

If you cannot speak in the moment, you may stay silent; if silence cannot protect, use skillful words. But you must not use the name of compassion to cover your own greed, fear and selfishness.

So keeping the precept against false speech is not merely guarding the letter of words; it is guarding a true heart, a kind heart, a heart that does not deceive.

Truth without compassion easily becomes a sharp blade; compassion without wisdom can also become foolishness. Therefore, with wisdom, let truth and compassion fulfill each other.

Where you can be true, be true; where you can be kind, be kind; when skillful means are unavoidable, let the heart not deceive others, and afterwards not deceive yourself.

Practicing like this, you come close to skillfulness.

🔶Plain Rendering

Keeping the precept against false speech is not only checking whether what you say is true or false; you must also look at the state of the heart behind the words.

If you know something is false and say it with the intent to deceive, so that others are harmed, that is false speech.

If it is to protect someone, not wanting them to suffer, with no intent to deceive or harm in your heart, and you use a skillful way of speaking — this needs careful discernment.

True words can hurt people; false words can also harm people. Between compassion and truth, you cannot simply say "just pick this one".

If you speak the truth only to vent your emotions, even if the content is true, it is not compassion.

If you speak kindly not to deceive, not for your own benefit, but only hoping to lessen the other person's pain, then you need to consider what the skillful way is.

If you can tell the truth without hurting them, tell the truth; if you can say it later, say it later; if you can say less, say less; there is no need to state every truth outright. If you truly cannot speak in the moment, you can stay silent; if silence cannot protect the other person, then use skillful words. But you must not use the name of "compassion" to cover up your own greed, fear or selfishness.

So keeping the precept against false speech does not ask people to cling rigidly to the literal truth of words;

it asks you to guard three kinds of heart: a truthful heart, a kind heart, and a heart that does not deceive. Truthful words without compassion easily become a knife that hurts; compassionate actions without wisdom can also become foolish. So you should use wisdom to let truth and compassion bring each other to completion. Where you can be truthful, be truthful; where you can be kind, be kind;

when you must use a skillful means, do not deceive others in your heart, and do not deceive yourself afterwards.

Practicing in this way comes close to the wisdom of skillful means.
