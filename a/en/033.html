No.33

Mr. Hsu, a Fellow Practitioner, Shares His Own Life Experience

From the karma of speech, family and the workplace toward repentance, helping others and self-reflection.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

Personal Account

Mr. Hsu, a fellow practitioner, shares his experience again: Some reflections on the karma of speech, cause and effect, and the path of practice In junior high school, I fell in with the wrong friends.

In those days I often played games in online voice chat rooms. Some of the people there came from broken families, and when we got together we loved to gossip and talk about right and wrong. When nothing was happening we felt bored, so we often stirred things up with our mouths.

I was the same back then.
Without wisdom, very foolish.

I created much karma of speech — harsh words, double-tongued talk, stirring up trouble — and besides speech, my mind was full of all kinds of mental karma; greed, anger and delusion were almost all there.

Looking back later, I felt that many things seemed to catch up with me very quickly.

I have a younger brother; there are two of us boys. In junior high, my father suddenly began scolding and humiliating me very easily.

I was just preparing for senior high. I studied in a work-study program, half work and half school, and later switched to night school.

Many things happened along the way.
At the time I did not understand; I only felt wronged.

I was not doing bad things on purpose. I just wanted to start working early to share the family's financial burden. I had no plan to go to university, so I started working in high school.

After graduating and starting work, I found the problems growing.

At work I often had poor communication with colleagues, and people reported on me behind my back. Although I met many kind helpers along the way, so many things seemed bound up with one thing — the mouth.

Sometimes it was someone else's words, sometimes a slip of my own.
One sentence can affect so much.
I used to have a beautiful voice.

In elementary school, the teacher often asked me to read our Chinese lessons aloud, and classmates liked to listen.

At twenty-one, I began to encounter the Dharma. At first it was simply repentance; apart from the Heart Sutra, I had not read many scriptures. But life was truly not going well; it seemed that whatever I wanted to do, I could not do. My father still scolded me often. Later I made a vow: to stop evil and cultivate good. Through the coming together of conditions, I came to know Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva.

I read the elder Venerable Master Mengcan saying that reciting the Ksitigarbha Sutra for one's parents can help them leave suffering and find happiness.

I believed this deeply, and so truly entered the Dharma. I began practicing meditation, reciting mantras, reciting sutras and making vows. The pain that had weighed on my heart for so long began slowly to loosen. That pain had once been so heavy that I almost could not go on. The real turning point was a very small thing. Once I helped a man who was nearly starving. I cooked for him and gave him several meals. It was strange. Afterwards I suddenly felt the pain in my heart let go.

I understood: sometimes helping others is also saving yourself. When a single thought turns, afflictions too may begin to loosen. Later I also came into contact with some practice methods. During practice, there were also some unusual experiences of body and mind. But I also understood: even if practice brings experiences, it does not mean life will have no more trials. Because trials came quickly.

Once, after practice, I had a very distinct physical sensation in my throat. The next day, while playing ball, I was suddenly struck in the throat. That injury had a very great effect. Later it even affected my neck, breathing and head, and brought long-term insomnia. Later still, while doing plumbing and electrical work on a construction site, I ran into a conflict. It was not that I hurt anyone with harsh words; the other man spoke improperly, and I only reasoned with him. Unexpectedly, he suddenly attacked me. Because my throat already had an old injury, after this my head felt very heavy and painful for half a year.

Even so, I did not take revenge, nor did I ask for compensation. Instead I recited sutras and dedicated the merit to the people who hurt me, hoping that they too could leave suffering and find happiness. I truly felt: the Dharma can sometimes slowly turn hatred into compassion. In February 2026, I began to repent more earnestly. I recited the holy names of Guanyin Bodhisattva and Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva, and also the Cundi mantra. During practice I also had some unusual feelings.

For me, I understand those experiences as the anger and attachment deep in my heart slowly rising to the surface and slowly being let go.

Afterwards I began working while doing good and sincerely blessing others — wishing everyone peace and health. Looking back now, my physical and mental condition has indeed improved a great deal. I have also had some dream experiences.

I once dreamed that Guanyin Bodhisattva bestowed sweet dew, and also dreamed that Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva appeared in an inconceivable way.

I cannot prove these things.
Some believe, some do not; that is fine.
Because this is my own experience.
But if I were to sum up the greatest lesson of the journey:
the mouth can hurt people, and it can also save people.
The mind can bind itself, and it can also set itself free.
If you once did wrong, you need not be trapped in the past forever.

If you are willing to turn back, willing to repent, willing to treat beings kindly — from that step on, perhaps your life has already begun to change.
