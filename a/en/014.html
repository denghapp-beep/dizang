No.14

What Is Space?

Through the relation between mind and place, reflecting on inner and outer, near and far, and freedom.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked the Bodhisattva: what is space?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"All dharmas are originally without form; the mind discriminates and so there are places — this is called space.

If the mind has no barriers, the ten directions are a single thought; if the mind clings, an inch of ground becomes a thousand miles.

Space is not truly existent, nor truly non-existent; it is a provisional name arising from conditions, appearing according to consciousness."

"Space is not the gap between walls; it is neither above nor below, neither far nor near. All forms are provisional names; what is called place is made by mind alone. If the mind is broad, the ten directions all open; if the mind is narrow, a single room becomes a prison. Beings take objects to be real, and so see inside and outside, near and far, high and low. If one can see that the mind is unobstructed, heaven and earth lie in the palm of the hand;

a single thought contains ten thousand worlds, and the ten thousand worlds are all reflections of one's own mind."

🔶Plain Rendering

All phenomena have no fixed, unchanging substance; it is only because our minds discriminate and grasp that we divide things into here and there, near and far, inside and outside — and call this "space".

So the nature of space is a perception that arises relatively. When your mind is open and unobstructed, even the vast ten directions are no more than one clear thought in the present moment; when your mind is filled with attachment, even a tiny place feels a thousand miles wide, with walls everywhere you turn.

Therefore, space is neither an objectively existing entity nor pure nothingness. It is more like a "temporary name and form" that appears through conditions and changes with the mind. All the nearness and distance, breadth and narrowness, and separation we feel are, at root, reflections of our inner state.

True space has never been only physical distance or direction. All the forms and positions you see are, in essence, labels and concepts given by the mind.

The mind is the final creator and definer.

When your mind is as vast as open sky, full of acceptance and awareness, heaven and earth in all ten directions open wide without obstruction, and everywhere there are ways out and possibilities.

When your mind is as narrow as the eye of a needle, stuffed with fear and prejudice, then even in open country you are as if trapped in an invisible cage, unable to move.

What traps you has never been the walls outside, but the walls you build within.

Because beings cling to outer circumstances as absolutely real, they are tightly bound by opposing ideas such as inside and outside, near and far, high and low, and from this come endless troubles and limits.

If you can see the truth and, through contemplation, let the mind rest in unobstructed awareness, you will experience a deep transformation:

the outer world, vast as it is, can be wholly held in your clear awareness, as plainly as something in the palm of your hand;

the single thought within, subtle as it is, can reflect the countless scenes of all worlds.

Then you will understand: the whole world you experience, magnificent or small, is the light and shadow shown in the mirror of your own mind's nature. Change the quality of the mind's mirror, and the world you see changes with it.
