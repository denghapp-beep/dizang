No.36

Since Buddhism Teaches Non-Attachment, Is There No Need to Bow to the Buddha?

Neither clinging to form nor slighting worship — reflecting on how reverence is lived out.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: the Diamond Sutra says that one who sees me by form or seeks me by sound walks a wrong path and cannot see the Tathagata. Does this mean there is no need to go to temples to pay respects to the Buddha and Bodhisattvas? Is bowing to the Buddha at a vihara also unnecessary? Is reverence in the heart enough?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"If you cling to the Buddha as being in form,
you are deluded by appearance;
if you abandon form and grow irreverent,
you fall into emptiness.
To see form without clinging to form,
to bow to the Buddha without clinging to the Buddha —
this is right practice.
If you take form for the Buddha,
you lose the Buddha;
if you use emptiness to destroy form,
you also lose the Buddha.

The Buddha is not in the hall,
nor apart from the hall;
the heart is the master,
conduct is the proof."

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

So the Bodhisattva means: the Buddha image is not the Buddha, but my attitude toward the image reflects my heart?

It is like loving someone: although love in the heart is enough, sometimes we still need to hug them, to write them a letter. In those five or ten minutes of bowing to the Buddha, we learn to focus and to let go. Is this understanding correct?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"The Buddha image is not the Buddha,
yet your heart toward the image
is the appearing of your heart.
If you slight the image,
it is not the image you slight,
but your own heart;
if you cling to the image,
it is not love for the Buddha,
but bondage to form.

In the time of bowing,
what matters is not long or short,
but whether you can,
within a single breath,
let go of discrimination.
To keep the mind unscattered for five minutes
surpasses a whole day with the mind absent."
