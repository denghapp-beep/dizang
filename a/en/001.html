The True Meaning of Practice

From counting recitations and prostrations back to the arising of each thought and the good done in daily life.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

One night I dreamed of Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva. In the dream I asked: if practice is not about how much good we do, how many times we bow to the Buddha, or even how many sutras we recite, then what exactly is it that we cultivate? Are we not born into this world in order to practice?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

"Practice is not ten thousand recitations of the mouth,
nor ten thousand bows of the hands;
nor counting merit like beads on an abacus,
nor clutching sutras like amulets.
One whose heart is free of greed, anger and delusion,
who is ever mindful of the suffering of beings — this is deep practice.
One who never bows before the Buddha,

yet whose every thought turns toward good, who pities others with a compassionate heart — this too is practice."

— "What you cultivate is nothing outside the Dharma; you cultivate your own original Buddha-nature. Like the lotus rising from the mud, like a mirror that was never dusty, all sutras, all holy images, all recitation and giving are but one gate: — to return you to your source, to see your nature and become Buddha." — "Ask not how many sutras or how many bows; ask whether your heart is stained with dust.

Seek not swift blessings; seek that every thought leave nothing to be ashamed of and be free of folly." (End of the dream teaching.)
