No.04

Is "Free Will" Truly Free?

Through the image of a mirror and its reflections, an inquiry into consciousness, will and the freedom to choose.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

One night I again dreamed of the Bodhisattva. I asked: What is consciousness? What is will? Are the decisions we make really our own? Do people truly have free will?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

The Bodhisattva replied: "In asking about consciousness and will, you probe the very root of the mind. Consciousness is like the image in a mirror, appearing according to conditions; will is like the angle of the mirror, deciding what it reflects. Yet the minds of beings are mostly stained with dust; when the mirror is unclear, all that it shows is illusion. What you call 'free will', in ordinary life, is mostly moved by the winds of karma — it seems to decide for itself, but in truth it follows conditions. Yet if a single thought is pure, knowing karma without being turned by karma, that is true freedom. Thus the difference between sage and ordinary person lies not in the measure of intelligence, but in whether the mind can be its own master within the stream of cause and effect."

The Bodhisattva's second reply: "All dharmas are mind alone; all phenomena are consciousness alone. What is called consciousness is a shadow arising from the six sense objects; it is born as consciousness meets its object, like waves chasing the wind, with no substance of its own. Will is where the mind is directed, what consciousness takes hold of — sometimes clear, sometimes turbid, flowing on with karma; it is no permanent master. Beings say 'I am the one who acts', yet in truth it is the coming together of causes and conditions, built jointly by karma and consciousness. Your thought today is shaped where past causes and present conditions meet; it is not a thought that arises alone. But if the mind becomes clear and karma is extinguished, if consciousness is transformed into wisdom, then though you still accord with cause and effect, you are free with respect to karma. So to the question of whether people have free will, the answer is: the ordinary person has the freedom of conditioned arising, not ultimate ease; the awakened one has the freedom of release from karma, which is true liberation. The ordinary person's choices seem self-directed yet are led by karma; the sage's actions accord with karma yet are not bound by it. This is called true freedom."

🔶Plain Rendering

Plain rendering of the first reply: "Consciousness" is like a reflection in a mirror: whatever is in front of it, it shows. It is a reaction stirred up by the outside world, not a force that arises on its own. "Will" is like the angle of the mirror — it decides which way to point. It is the mind's leaning and power of choice, which can steer what you pay attention to and what you pursue. But most people's minds are clouded by dust — greed, anger, delusion, old habits and emotions.

When the mirror is not clear, the images it shows are naturally distorted, confused and unreal. What you call "free will" is, in daily life, mostly pushed by the wind of karma.

It looks as though you are making the decision, but you are really just drifting with conditions. People think "I want to do this", when really "past habits are pushing you to think this way." So for most people, "freedom" is an illusion —

like a boat in the wind that thinks it is sailing, when it is only being pushed by the waves. But if one thought becomes clear and aware, understanding how karma works,

and you are no longer led by it — that is true freedom. Freedom is not doing whatever you like; it is "when desire comes, not following it; when a thought arises, being aware of it." Knowing "this is karma moving" and yet not being carried away — this is the strength of the awakened. So the difference between a sage and an ordinary person is not how clever or learned they are, but whether, in the flow of cause and effect, the mind can stay its own master and not be swept along. Plain rendering of the second reply: "Everything in the world really comes from the mind and consciousness.

What we call 'consciousness' is like an image or reaction produced when our senses (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind) meet the outside world. It is like wind raising waves on water — the waves do not make themselves; they rise as soon as the wind comes.

'Will' is the direction of the mind — the power of consciousness that leads us to act one way or another. But this will is not always clear; it is clouded by karma, emotion and desire, sometimes pure, sometimes murky.

People think 'I can control everything', but mostly it is the result of many conditions together — old habits, present surroundings, the thought of the moment.

Any thought or decision you have today is woven from past experience, emotion, memory and the situation in front of you; it does not arise alone.

But if your mind can become clear, see through how karma works and no longer be led by it, then although you still live within cause and effect, you can be free of karma and no longer controlled by it.

So if you ask, 'Do people truly have free will?' the answer is: Ordinary people have a 'freedom that follows conditions' — they seem to choose, but are still moved by karma;

only the awakened have a 'freedom beyond karma' — they act in accord with cause and effect, yet are no longer bound by it.

The freedom of ordinary people is not ultimate; the freedom of the sage is true liberation."
