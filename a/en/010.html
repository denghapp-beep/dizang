No.10

[A Question of Shared Karma] Soaring Housing Prices, Rat Races and Lying Flat

Under collective pressures of living, reflecting on personal action and shared change.

Author's record · translated; the Chinese original is authoritative

Open in reading mode →

🔶Inquiry to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

I asked Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva: In Taiwan today, young people work hard yet cannot afford a home, cannot afford to marry, cannot afford to have children.

Many are crushed every day by mortgages, childcare costs and the workplace rat race until they can hardly breathe.

Some choose to lie flat, some choose to emigrate, some push on until body and mind collapse. Compassionate Bodhisattva, in this environment where shared karma is made manifest, how many years of hard practice by ordinary people, how many people awakening, before this collective karmic obstacle of housing prices and living pressure can truly loosen? Or is this generation destined to carry this whole round before the next generation can breathe easier?

If it is to loosen, what is the most effective small act of "turning karma" a person can do now — not personal practice, but something that can affect the collective?

🔶The Bodhisattva's Teaching

Beings sigh that they are born into a turbid age; this is not one person's karma, but the gathering of many conditions together.

Mountains, rivers and the great earth have no wealth or poverty, no honor or lowliness; only the discriminations of many hearts make high and low.

The dearness of houses is not the fault of the houses; it is the continuing stream of hearts that fear poverty, dread old age, and are afraid of losing what they rely on.

When shared karma is formed, one generation cannot lay it down; when a shared vow is formed, neither can one person turn it alone.

Yet let no being belittle a single clear thought, let none abandon a single measure of right conduct.

When one person does not deceive their own heart, a hundred feel its breeze; when a hundred do not deceive the world, a thousand change its customs.

You ask how many generations until it turns? It lies not in years, but in people willing to wake. If one generation sleeps, a hundred generations labor; if one generation awakens, a hundred karmic burdens loosen. It is not that you must carry it all before it lightens; but someone must first refuse to kneel before fear, and refuse to bow before habit.

🔶Plain Rendering

Beings lament how hard it is to be born in a troubled age. This is not the karma of one person, but the result of many conditions coming together.

Mountains, rivers and the earth have no rich or poor, noble or humble in themselves; it is only because of the distinctions and attachments in people's hearts that high and low appear.

High house prices are not the fault of bricks and tiles. They are the image of people's deep fears — of poverty, of having no one to rely on in old age, of being uprooted and homeless — continuing without end and piling up layer upon layer.

The force of shared karma, once formed, cannot be fully laid down by one generation; and when a shared vow is about to take shape, it cannot be fully turned by the strength of one person. Yet beings must never underestimate the power of one clear thought, nor give up even a little upright conduct. When one person does not deceive their own heart, a hundred people feel that fresh breeze;

when a hundred people do not deceive the world (that is, do not deceive others), a thousand people can gradually change old customs.

You ask: how many generations must pass before things change? The answer lies not in the number of years, but in when people's hearts awaken. If one generation is content to sleep, a hundred generations must bear the heavy load; if one generation rises and awakens, the shackles of shared karma naturally loosen. You are not necessarily destined to carry every hardship before later generations can enjoy ease. But someone must be the first to stand up — no longer kneeling before fear, no longer giving in to old habits.
